# Cat Care Basics

## Feeding

Most adult cats do well on two meals a day, morning and evening, with portions sized to their weight (a typical 10-pound indoor cat needs roughly 200-250 calories per day). Cats are obligate carnivores — their diet must contain animal protein. Dry kibble is convenient but lower in moisture; wet food helps with hydration, especially for cats prone to urinary issues.

Free-feeding works for some cats but can cause obesity in lower-activity indoor cats. Schedule feeding works better for weight control and bonding.

## Litter box

Rule of thumb: one box per cat plus one extra. So two cats need three boxes. Boxes should be scooped at least once a day; the entire box should be emptied and washed weekly.

A cat suddenly avoiding the litter box is almost always a medical or stress signal — urinary blockages, arthritis, or a new household stressor. This is not a training issue and should be checked by a vet, not punished.

## Indoor vs outdoor

Indoor cats live significantly longer (15+ years vs 5-7 for outdoor cats) due to reduced exposure to traffic, predators, and disease. Indoor cats need extra enrichment — climbing surfaces, window perches, daily play sessions — to compensate for the loss of hunting and territory patrolling.

## Grooming

Short-haired cats self-groom and only need occasional brushing (once a week). Long-haired breeds (Persians, Maine Coons) need brushing 3-4 times per week to prevent painful matting. Cats rarely need baths — only if they get into something they can't lick off.

## Sleep

Cats sleep 12-16 hours a day, mostly in short naps. This is normal — concern only arises if a cat stops eating, hides constantly, or stops grooming itself.
