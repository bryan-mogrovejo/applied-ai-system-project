# Grooming Schedule

## Bath frequency

**Dogs:**
- Short-coated breeds (Beagles, Boxers, Labradors): every 1-3 months
- Medium-coated (Golden Retrievers, Shepherds): every 4-6 weeks
- Curly or non-shedding (Poodles, Doodles, Bichons): every 3-4 weeks, paired with professional grooming
- Working dogs that get muddy frequently: as needed, but use a gentle moisturizing shampoo

Over-bathing strips natural oils and causes dry, itchy skin. Most healthy dogs do not need weekly baths.

**Cats:** rarely need baths. They self-groom. Bathe only if the cat gets into something toxic or sticky, or for medical reasons (flea bath, skin condition).

## Brushing

Brushing distributes natural oils, removes loose fur, and lets the owner notice lumps, ticks, or skin issues early.

- Short-haired dogs and cats: weekly
- Medium-haired: 2-3 times per week
- Long-haired (Maine Coons, Persians, Collies, Goldens): daily during shedding season; 3-4x weekly otherwise
- Double-coated breeds (Huskies, German Shepherds): undercoat rake during seasonal shed (spring and fall)

## Nail trimming

- Dogs: every 3-4 weeks. If you can hear nails clicking on hard floors, they're too long.
- Cats: every 2-3 weeks for indoor cats (outdoor cats wear nails down naturally)

Trim only the white tip — avoid the pink "quick" inside the nail, which contains blood vessels and nerves. If unsure, have a groomer or vet show the technique once.

## Ear cleaning

- Floppy-eared breeds (Cocker Spaniels, Basset Hounds): weekly check, clean as needed
- Erect-eared breeds: monthly check
- Cats: rarely need cleaning unless mites are present

Use a vet-approved ear cleaner — never insert cotton swabs into the ear canal.

## Teeth

Dental disease is one of the most common pet health issues. Brush teeth 3-4 times per week with pet-specific toothpaste (human toothpaste contains xylitol, which is toxic). Dental chews and water additives help but do not replace brushing.
