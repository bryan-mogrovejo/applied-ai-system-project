# Dog Behavior and Training

## Exercise-behavior link

Most "behavior problems" in dogs are exercise and enrichment problems. A dog that destroys furniture, barks excessively, or pulls on leash is often under-exercised or under-stimulated. Before reaching for behavioral fixes, check that the dog is getting:

- Daily aerobic exercise appropriate to the breed
- Mental stimulation (training, puzzle feeders, sniff walks)
- Social contact (with humans and, when appropriate, other dogs)
- A predictable daily routine

Tired dogs sleep. Bored dogs get creative.

## Training basics

Dogs learn through consequences. Reward what you want, ignore or redirect what you don't. Positive reinforcement (treats, praise, play) builds trust and works long-term. Punishment-based methods (yelling, leash pops, shock collars) often suppress symptoms but increase fear and aggression.

Foundational commands every dog should know:
- **Sit** — useful for greeting people calmly
- **Stay** — for safety at doors and curbs
- **Come (recall)** — potentially life-saving if a dog gets loose
- **Leave it** — prevents eating dangerous things on walks
- **Drop it** — for taking objects out of the mouth safely

Short, frequent training sessions (5 minutes, 2-3 times a day) work better than one long session.

## Common behavior issues

**Pulling on leash**: stop walking the moment the leash tightens. Resume only when slack returns. The dog learns that pulling delays progress.

**Barking at the door**: most door barking is excitement or alert behavior, not aggression. Train an incompatible behavior — go to a mat and lie down — when the doorbell rings.

**Separation anxiety**: signs include destructive chewing only when alone, urinating despite being house-trained, or excessive barking when left. Treat with gradual desensitization, not punishment after the fact (the dog won't connect the punishment to the act).

**Resource guarding**: a dog protecting food, toys, or space. Do not punish — this often makes it worse. Trade up: approach with a higher-value treat so the dog learns approach = good things.

## Socialization window

Puppies have a critical socialization window between 3 and 14 weeks. Positive exposure during this period to people, dogs, surfaces, sounds, and environments prevents many adult fear-based behaviors. After 14 weeks, socialization still helps but is harder.
