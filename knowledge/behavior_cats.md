# Cat Behavior and Enrichment

## Cats are predators, not small dogs

Cat behavior is shaped by their hunting instincts. Many "problem" behaviors are normal hunting/territorial behavior expressed in the wrong place. The fix is usually environmental, not behavioral correction.

## Daily play

Indoor cats need at least two 10-15 minute structured play sessions per day, ideally before meals. Wand toys (feathers, string) that mimic prey movement work better than balls or laser pointers. Laser pointers can frustrate cats because they can never "catch" the prey — always end a laser session with a physical toy the cat can grab.

A cat that gets enough play during the day is far less likely to wake the owner at 4 AM with the "zoomies."

## Scratching

Scratching is not destructive behavior — it's territorial marking, claw maintenance, and stretching. Cats will scratch. The goal is to redirect, not eliminate.

Provide multiple scratching surfaces:
- A tall vertical post (taller than the cat's full stretch — at least 30 inches)
- A horizontal scratching pad
- Different materials (sisal rope, cardboard, carpet) — cats have preferences

Place posts near where the cat already wants to scratch (often near sleeping spots and entrances). Reward use with treats or praise.

Declawing is amputation of the last bone of each toe and is banned in many countries. It causes lifelong pain and behavior issues.

## Litter box problems

A cat that suddenly stops using the box is communicating something:
- **Medical**: urinary tract infection, blockage (life-threatening in male cats), arthritis making the box hard to enter
- **Box issues**: dirty, wrong type of litter, location too noisy, hooded box trapping odors
- **Stress**: new pet, moved furniture, new person in the home, neighbor cat visible through windows

First step: vet visit to rule out medical causes. Never punish litter box accidents — it increases stress and worsens the problem.

## Aggression and play biting

Kittens raised without littermates often miss out on bite inhibition lessons. If a cat bites or scratches during play, freeze (don't pull away — that triggers prey drive) and end the session. Provide appropriate toys for biting and never use hands as toys.

## Multi-cat households

Cats are not naturally social with non-related cats. Introduce new cats slowly over weeks: scent swapping → seeing each other through a barrier → supervised meetings → free access. Rushing introductions causes lasting tension.
