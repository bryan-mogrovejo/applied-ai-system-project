# Pet Nutrition and Toxic Foods

## Toxic foods (common emergencies)

These foods cause serious illness or death and should never be fed:

**Toxic to dogs and cats:**
- Chocolate (especially dark chocolate and baker's chocolate)
- Grapes and raisins (cause kidney failure even in small amounts)
- Onions, garlic, leeks, chives (in any form — raw, cooked, powdered)
- Xylitol (artificial sweetener in gum, peanut butter, baked goods)
- Macadamia nuts
- Alcohol and caffeine
- Cooked bones (splinter and cause internal damage)
- Raw bread dough (expands and produces alcohol in the stomach)

**Additional cat-specific toxins:**
- Lilies (even pollen on fur is fatal — affects kidneys)
- Tuna as a regular meal (mercury, thiamine deficiency)

**Additional dog-specific concerns:**
- Avocado pit and skin
- Large quantities of fatty foods (pancreatitis risk)

## Portion sizes

Feeding amount depends on weight, age, activity level, and whether the pet is spayed/neutered.

Quick reference for adult dogs (per day, split into 2 meals):
- Toy breed (5 lb): 1/4 to 1/2 cup dry food
- Small (15 lb): 3/4 to 1 cup
- Medium (30 lb): 1.5 to 2 cups
- Large (60 lb): 2.5 to 3 cups
- Giant (100 lb): 4+ cups

Adult cats: roughly 20 calories per pound of body weight per day.

## Treats

Treats should make up no more than 10% of daily calories. Use a portion of regular kibble as training treats to avoid weight gain.

## Switching foods

Transition between foods over 7-10 days to avoid stomach upset: 25% new / 75% old for 3 days, then 50/50, then 75/25, then full new food.
