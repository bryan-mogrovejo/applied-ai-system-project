# Pet Medication Guidelines

## Timing and consistency

For most prescription medications, consistency matters more than the exact hour. A medication labeled "twice daily" should be given roughly 12 hours apart, not at random times. Pick two anchor times that fit the owner's schedule (e.g., 8 AM and 8 PM) and stick to them.

For "once daily" meds, the same time each day is ideal — drug levels in the bloodstream stay more stable.

## With or without food

This depends on the medication:

**Give with food** to avoid stomach upset:
- NSAIDs (carprofen / Rimadyl, meloxicam, deracoxib)
- Steroids (prednisone, prednisolone)
- Most antibiotics (amoxicillin, clavamox, doxycycline)

**Give on an empty stomach** (food blocks absorption):
- Thyroid medications (levothyroxine)
- Some heart medications (consult vet)

When in doubt, the medication label or pharmacist instructions are authoritative.

## Common medications and what they're for

- **Carprofen / Rimadyl**: NSAID for pain and inflammation, usually for arthritis or post-surgery
- **Apoquel**: anti-itch for allergies
- **Heartgard / Interceptor**: monthly heartworm prevention
- **Frontline / Bravecto**: flea and tick prevention
- **Insulin**: diabetic pets, given with meals at consistent times
- **Phenobarbital**: seizure control, requires strict timing

## Pilling tips

Pill pockets (soft treats with a hollow center) work for most dogs. For cats, pilling is harder — gently tilt the head up, place the pill at the back of the tongue, hold the mouth closed briefly, then offer water or wet food. A pill gun (plastic plunger) is useful for cats that bite.

## Missed doses

Never double up. If a dose is missed by less than half the interval, give it; otherwise skip and resume the next scheduled dose. For seizure or insulin medications, contact the vet — these have stricter rules.

## Storage

Keep medications in their original containers, away from heat and humidity. Bathroom medicine cabinets are often the worst place — humidity from showers degrades pills. A bedroom drawer is better.
