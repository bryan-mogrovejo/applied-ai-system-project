# Small Pets: Rabbits and Birds

## Rabbits

Rabbits are not low-maintenance "starter pets." They are social, intelligent animals that need:
- A diet of unlimited grass hay (timothy or orchard), fresh leafy greens daily, and a small portion of pellets
- At least 3-4 hours of exercise outside the cage every day
- A spayed or neutered companion (rabbits are highly social and isolation causes stress)
- A litter box (yes, rabbits can be litter-trained)

Iceberg lettuce, beans, and bread are harmful. Avoid sugary fruits except as occasional treats.

A rabbit grinding its teeth softly while relaxed is content. Loud teeth grinding can mean pain — see a vet.

## Birds

Care depends heavily on species. General guidelines for common companion birds (budgies, cockatiels, conures):

- **Diet**: A pellet base (60-70%) plus fresh vegetables and small amounts of fruit. Seeds alone cause obesity and nutritional deficiencies.
- **Cage time**: Birds need several hours per day of out-of-cage time in a bird-safe room (no ceiling fans, no open windows, no Teflon cookware nearby — Teflon fumes are lethal to birds).
- **Social needs**: Most parrots are flock animals and become depressed in isolation. Either provide a same-species companion or commit to multiple hours of daily human interaction.
- **Sleep**: Birds need 10-12 hours of dark, quiet sleep. A covered cage or dedicated sleep cage helps.

Common toxic items for birds: avocado, chocolate, caffeine, alcohol, salt, and any aerosol sprays (air fresheners, hair spray, non-stick cookware fumes).

## General rule for small pets

Small does not mean simple. Always research species-specific needs before adopting.
