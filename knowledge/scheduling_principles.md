# Daily Scheduling Principles for Pet Care

These principles help explain why a daily plan is structured the way it is.

## Time-of-day priorities

**Morning (6-9 AM):**
- Potty break first thing — most dogs need to relieve themselves within 5-10 minutes of waking
- Walk before breakfast for healthy adult dogs (aids digestion, reduces hunger pulling on leash)
- Breakfast and morning medications (most meds are better tolerated with food)
- Cats: morning play and feeding sets a routine that reduces 4 AM wake-ups

**Midday (11 AM - 2 PM):**
- Brief potty break for dogs left alone
- Light snack or training session
- Best time for grooming sessions — pets are typically calm in the middle of the day

**Late afternoon / evening (5-8 PM):**
- Main exercise session (longest walk, fetch, play)
- Dinner about 30-60 minutes after exercise (not immediately — risk of bloat in large dogs)
- Evening medications

**Night (9-11 PM):**
- Final potty break before bed
- Quiet time, no high-energy play within an hour of bedtime

## Why priority matters within a time slot

When two tasks are scheduled at the same time, "high priority" generally means safety-critical or time-sensitive:
- Medications must happen on schedule (drug levels in the bloodstream depend on consistency)
- Diabetic feeding must happen with insulin — they're paired
- Senior pet pain medication should not be delayed

Lower-priority tasks (grooming, optional enrichment, bath) can shift to fit around the high-priority anchors.

## Avoiding common scheduling mistakes

**Walks too soon after meals**: dogs can develop gastric torsion (life-threatening) if they exercise vigorously right after eating, especially deep-chested breeds. Wait 60+ minutes.

**Inconsistent meal times**: pets thrive on routine. Wildly different feeding times each day stresses cats especially and can cause begging behavior in dogs.

**Stacking medications at one time**: some medications interact or cause stomach upset when combined. Spread medications across the day when possible, and follow vet instructions about which can be given together.

**Ignoring the pet's natural rhythm**: cats are crepuscular (active at dawn and dusk). Scheduling a play session at 3 PM is fighting their biology. Aim for early morning and just before dinner.

## Conflicts and tradeoffs

When two important tasks land at the same time (e.g., morning meds and morning walk), the typical resolution is:
- Give the medication first if it requires food (have a small treat ready)
- Walk second
- Full breakfast after the walk

This sequence respects medication timing, exercise-before-eating health practice, and gives the dog a clear routine.
