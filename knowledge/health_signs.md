# Warning Signs and When to Call a Vet

This guide is informational. When in doubt, call a vet — many emergencies look mild at first.

## Emergency signs (go to vet immediately)

**Any pet:**
- Difficulty breathing, blue-tinged gums, gasping
- Collapse or sudden inability to stand
- Seizures lasting longer than 2 minutes, or multiple seizures in 24 hours
- Bloated, hard abdomen with retching but nothing coming up (gastric torsion in dogs — fatal within hours)
- Suspected poisoning (chocolate, grapes, xylitol, antifreeze, rodenticide, lilies)
- Severe trauma (hit by car, fall from height, dog attack)
- Heavy bleeding that doesn't slow with pressure
- Difficulty urinating, especially in male cats (urinary blockage — fatal within 24-48 hours)
- Heatstroke signs: heavy panting, drooling, vomiting, collapse after sun/heat exposure
- Eye injuries or sudden severe eye redness

## Same-day vet visit

- Vomiting more than 2-3 times in a day, or with blood
- Diarrhea with blood or lasting more than 24 hours
- Refusing food for more than 24 hours (cats are especially sensitive — fatty liver disease can develop quickly)
- Drinking and urinating much more than usual
- Sudden lameness or limping
- Lethargy combined with not eating
- Coughing fits, especially with foam or blood

## Schedule a vet appointment within a week

- Gradual weight loss or gain
- Persistent itching or hair loss
- Bad breath (often dental disease)
- Lumps or bumps under the skin (most are benign, but vets should check)
- Behavior changes lasting more than a few days (hiding, aggression, restlessness)
- Stiffness or trouble jumping, especially in older pets

## Common vital signs (resting)

**Dogs:**
- Heart rate: 60-140 bpm (smaller dogs faster)
- Respiratory rate: 10-30 breaths/min
- Temperature: 101-102.5°F (38.3-39.2°C)

**Cats:**
- Heart rate: 140-220 bpm
- Respiratory rate: 20-30 breaths/min
- Temperature: 100.5-102.5°F (38-39.2°C)

A pet temperature above 103°F or below 99°F warrants a vet call.

## Subtle but serious cat signs

Cats hide illness exceptionally well. By the time symptoms are obvious, disease is often advanced. Watch for:
- Eating less, even slightly
- Drinking from the faucet or unusual water sources
- Sleeping in different places than usual
- Reduced grooming (matted fur, oily coat)
- Less interaction with the family

These small changes are often the first sign of kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, or cancer. Early detection extends life significantly.

## Disclaimer

This document is general guidance. It is not a substitute for veterinary diagnosis. Always consult a vet for your specific pet.
